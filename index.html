THE UNIVERSE

The observable universe contains an enormous collection of galaxies, stars, planets, nebulae and other cosmic structures.

GALAXIES

Galaxies are enormous systems containing stars, planets, gas, dust and dark matter. Our home galaxy is the Milky Way.

STARS

Stars are giant balls of extremely hot plasma. Their energy comes from nuclear fusion occurring deep within their cores.

PLANETS

Planets are celestial bodies that orbit stars. Some are rocky worlds while others are enormous gas or ice giants.

NEBULAE

Nebulae are enormous clouds of gas and dust. Some are places where new stars are born.

DARK MATTER

Dark matter cannot be directly observed through ordinary light, but its gravitational effects influence galaxies and cosmic structures.

DARK ENERGY

Dark energy is the name given to the unknown component associated with the accelerated expansion of the universe.

EVENT HORIZON

A black hole is an object whose gravity is so strong that beyond its event horizon, light cannot escape.

SINGULARITY

Classical general relativity predicts a region of extreme gravitational curvature at the center of a black hole.

EVENT HORIZON

The event horizon is a boundary beyond which signals cannot escape to distant observers.

ACCRETION DISK

Matter falling toward a black hole can form a rapidly rotating disk that becomes extremely hot and luminous.

THE EARLY UNIVERSE

FIRST STARS

After the early universe cooled sufficiently, the first generations of stars began forming.

GALAXIES

THE MILKY WAY

Our galaxy formed and evolved into the spiral galaxy we observe today.

EARTH

Earth formed around 4.5 billion years ago around the young Sun.

HUMANITY

Humans eventually developed technology capable of observing and exploring space.
