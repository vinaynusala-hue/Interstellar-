WHAT IS THE MOVIE ABOUT?

Interstellar follows a group of astronauts who travel beyond our solar system in search of a new home for humanity. The journey combines family, sacrifice, exploration and theoretical physics.

WHY IS IT SPECIAL?

The film connects enormous cosmic ideas with deeply personal human emotions. Time, gravity, distance and love become part of the same story.

DEEP SPACE VEHICLE
THE ENDURANCE

The Endurance is the spacecraft used by the crew during their interstellar expedition. Its rotating structure visually represents the concept of artificial gravity.

CANDIDATE WORLDS
THREE WORLDS

The Endurance crew investigates worlds located within the system reached through the wormhole.

MILLER'S PLANET

A water-covered world orbiting very close to Gargantua. Its proximity to the black hole results in extreme gravitational time dilation in the film.

MANN'S PLANET

A frozen world investigated by the crew after the astronaut Dr. Mann transmits information suggesting that the planet could support human life.

EDMUNDS' PLANET

A distant candidate world associated with Dr. Edmunds. Its potential habitability becomes important to the mission's ultimate future.

A SHORTCUT THROUGH SPACETIME
THE WORMHOLE

A wormhole is a hypothetical connection between distant regions of spacetime. In Interstellar, the wormhole near Saturn provides the crew with a route toward another galaxy.

THE PHYSICS
THE SCIENCE

Interstellar incorporates concepts from modern physics, especially general relativity. Some elements are grounded in established theory, while others are speculative science fiction.

BLACK HOLES

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity becomes extraordinarily strong. Its event horizon marks a boundary beyond which light cannot escape.

GRAVITATIONAL LENSING

Massive objects can curve spacetime and bend the path of light. This is called gravitational lensing and strongly influenced the visual appearance of Gargantua.

TIME DILATION

Relativity predicts that clocks can run at different rates depending on gravitational field strength and relative motion.

GENERAL RELATIVITY

Einstein's theory describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy.

WORMHOLES

Wormholes appear as mathematical possibilities in certain spacetime models. Traversable wormholes, however, remain hypothetical.

HIGHER DIMENSIONS

The film's tesseract sequence explores a speculative higher-dimensional representation of time and gravity.

BEYOND THE MOVIE
SPACE FACTS
01

LIGHT-YEARS

A light-year is a unit of distance: the distance light travels through vacuum in one year.

02

NEUTRON STARS

These extremely dense stellar remnants can pack more mass than the Sun into a sphere roughly the size of a city.

03

SUPERNOVAE

Massive stars can end their lives in enormous explosions that spread elements into space.

04

EXOPLANETS

Exoplanets are planets orbiting stars outside our solar system. Thousands have been confirmed.

05

GALAXIES

Galaxies contain stars, gas, dust, dark matter and other structures bound together by gravity.

06

EVENT HORIZON

The event horizon is the boundary around a black hole beyond which information cannot escape to distant observers.

07

DARK MATTER

Dark matter is inferred from gravitational effects even though it does not appear to interact with light in the same way ordinary matter does.

08

COSMIC SCALE

Light from distant galaxies can travel for billions of years before reaching our telescopes.
